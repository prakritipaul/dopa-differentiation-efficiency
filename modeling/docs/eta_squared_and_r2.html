Eta² and R² are the same quantity

They differ only in what counts as “the model.” Here is the identity, why r loses its sign on the way to R², and what that means for the pool-association numbers in the feature-importance tables.

One formula, three names

Every one of these answers the same question — what fraction of a variable’s spread does my model account for?

ratio = ( SStotal − SSresidual ) / SStotal
explained variance ÷ total variance

The only thing that changes between them is what the model predicts, and therefore what counts as a residual.

R²continuousthe fitted linedistance to the line
η²categoricalthat group’s meandistance to your group’s mean

That is the whole difference. η² is R² where the model is “predict every point by the mean of its group.”

Not analogous — identical

The code in 008_technical_covariate_associations.py is the formula above, with ss_within as the residual:

# each point's residual = distance from its own pool's mean
ss_total    = ((values - grand_mean) ** 2).sum()
group_means = values.groupby(pool).transform("mean")
ss_within   = ((values - group_means) ** 2).sum()
return (ss_total - ss_within) / ss_total

Now regress the same feature on 9 pool dummy variables by ordinary least squares. The fitted values are the group means — that is what OLS on dummies gives you. So the R² of that regression equals η², to the last decimal. One-way ANOVA is linear regression on dummies.

Where r fits, and where its sign goes

For simple linear regression with one continuous predictor:

R² = r²

That is the entire relationship. And it explains the loss: r = −0.8 and r = +0.8 both give R² = 0.64. Squaring destroys direction. R² tells you how much variance is explained, never which way.

η² inherits that, which is fine here — pool is nominal. There is no ordering, so “the feature increases with pool” would be meaningless. You cannot compute r against a categorical variable at all.

√η² — the correlation ratio — puts it back on |r|’s scale. PC2’s 0.764 becomes 0.87.

phat_NB sits below chance

At 0.037 it scores lower than a random feature would. That is a stronger claim than the “Low” bucket label carries: there is no detectable pool structure in it at all. Every other feature in the table sits far above the floor (p < 0.0005).

It also shows the bucket thresholds are generous at the bottom — “Low” is < 0.15, but chance alone is ~0.057, so that band is only about 2× chance.

Two things worth carrying

η² is more general than r — in one direction

r detects only linear association. η² detects any difference in group means. Bin a continuous predictor with a U-shaped relationship and r ≈ 0 while η² is large. It catches curvature r is blind to — at the cost of discarding order.

η² is upward-biased, like R²

Add groups and it only rises — that is the chance floor. The bias-corrected analogue is ω², ANOVA’s answer to adjusted R². Using ω² would subtract the floor automatically, and is arguably what the pipeline should report — though with everything but phat_NB far above it, no conclusion here would change.
